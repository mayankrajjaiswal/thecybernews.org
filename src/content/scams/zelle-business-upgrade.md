## What is it?
The Zelle "Business Upgrade" scam targets people selling items online (like on Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, or OfferUp). The scammer poses as a buyer, agrees to your price, and offers to pay via Zelle. However, they claim the payment failed because your Zelle account needs a "Business Upgrade" to receive their funds.

## How it works (The Teardown)
1. **The Hook:** You list an item for sale. The scammer messages you immediately, agreeing to your full price without haggling. They insist on paying with Zelle.
2. **The Fake Email:** The scammer asks for your email address to send the Zelle payment. Shortly after, you receive an email that *looks* like it's from Zelle (e.g., `zelle-support@gmail.com`). 
3. **The Trap:** The email claims the buyer sent the money, but it is "pending." It states your account has a limit and you must upgrade to a "Zelle Business Account" to receive the funds.
4. **The Demand:** The email (and the scammer via chat) tells you the buyer needs to send an *additional* $300 to trigger the upgrade. The buyer sends you a fake screenshot showing they sent the extra $300.
5. **The Theft:** The scammer tells you to refund them the $300 since they "helped you upgrade." You send $300 of your real money from your actual bank account. The initial payment never existed.

## How to identify this scam
* **Fake Zelle Emails:** Official Zelle emails will NEVER come from `@gmail.com`, `@yahoo.com`, or other free providers. They come from `@zellepay.com`.
* **No "Business Upgrade" exists:** Zelle does not hold funds pending an account upgrade. If someone sends you money on Zelle, it appears in your bank account instantly. 
* **Overpayment:** Any time a stranger sends you "extra" money and asks you to send a portion of it back, it is a scam.

## How to avoid it
* **Check your bank app, not your email:** Never trust an email claiming you received money. Log into your actual banking app. If the money isn't there, it wasn't sent.
* **Cash is king:** When selling items locally on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist, always insist on cash in person.
* **Keep communication in the app:** Scammers always try to move the conversation to text messages or email so the platform (like Facebook) can't monitor or ban them.

## What to do if you are affected
* **Contact your bank:** If you sent money to the scammer, contact your bank's fraud department immediately. Because Zelle transactions are authorized by you, getting the money back is very difficult, but you must report it.
* **Report the scammer:** Report the buyer's profile on Facebook Marketplace or the platform you used.
* **Report the email:** Mark the fake Zelle email as "Phishing" or "Spam" in your email provider.
