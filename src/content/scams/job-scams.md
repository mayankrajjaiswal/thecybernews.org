## What is it?
The Fake Job Offer scam targets people looking for remote work or side hustles. Scammers post incredibly attractive job listings on LinkedIn, Indeed, or send unsolicited text messages offering a "work from home" position that pays $50/hour for basic data entry. Their actual goal is to steal your Social Security Number during the "onboarding" process, or trick you into cashing a fraudulent check.

## How it works (The Teardown)
1. **The Outreach:** You receive a WhatsApp or text message: *"Hi, I'm a recruiter for [Well-Known Company]. We saw your resume and want to offer you a remote data entry job paying $1,500 a week."*
2. **The "Interview":** You reply, and they conduct a brief, text-only interview over an app like Telegram, Signal, or Skype. You get the job immediately.
3. **The Check:** They tell you that you need to purchase a specific MacBook and software to do the job. They email you a digital check for $3,000 and say: *"Deposit this into your bank account. Keep $500 as a sign-on bonus, and use the remaining $2,500 to buy the laptop from our approved vendor."*
4. **The Theft:** You deposit the check and send the $2,500 to the "vendor" (which is actually just the scammer). Three days later, your bank realizes the original check was completely fake and bounces it. 
5. **The Aftermath:** The bank deducts the $3,000 from your account, leaving you in the negative. The scammer vanishes with the $2,500 of your real money that you sent them.

## How to identify this scam
* **Too Good to Be True:** No legitimate company pays $50/hour for entry-level, remote data entry with no experience required.
* **Text-Only Interviews:** Real companies do not conduct interviews exclusively via text message on Telegram or WhatsApp.
* **Buying your own equipment:** A real company will mail you a laptop. They will *never* ask you to deposit a check and buy the equipment yourself from a "special vendor."

## How to avoid it
* **Never pay to work:** If a job requires you to spend your own money upfront to buy software, equipment, or training, it is almost certainly a scam.
* **Verify the recruiter:** If someone claims to be a recruiter for a major company like Google or Amazon, find their official profile on LinkedIn. Call the actual company's HR department and verify the recruiter actually works there.
* **Beware of checks:** Never accept a check from someone you haven't met in person, especially if they ask you to return a portion of the funds to them.

## What to do if you are affected
1. **Contact your bank:** If you deposited a fake check, call your bank's fraud department immediately. Be honest about what happened so they don't flag your account for intentional fraud.
2. **Freeze your credit:** If you filled out a "W-4" or provided your Social Security Number during the fake onboarding process, you must freeze your credit with the three major bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) immediately to prevent identity theft.
