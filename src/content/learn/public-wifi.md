## The Invisible Threat
When you connect to your Wi-Fi at home, you have a password that encrypts the airwaves, keeping your data safe from your neighbors. 

When you connect to a free, public Wi-Fi network at a coffee shop, hotel, or airport, there is usually no password required to connect. This means the airwaves are **unencrypted**. Anyone sitting in that same coffee shop with a laptop and free hacking software can literally pluck your internet traffic out of the air and read it as it floats by.

## The "Evil Twin" Attack
Hackers don't just passively listen to public Wi-Fi; they actively set traps.
A hacker might sit in a Starbucks and use their laptop to broadcast a fake Wi-Fi network named "Starbucks_Free_WiFi". Because the signal is stronger than the real router in the back room, your phone automatically connects to the hacker's laptop instead. 

Now, the hacker controls your connection to the internet. If you try to go to your bank's website, the hacker can intercept the request, show you a fake login page, and steal your password.

## 3 Rules for Using Public Wi-Fi Safely

If you must use public Wi-Fi while traveling or working remotely, follow these absolute rules:

### 1. Never Do Your Banking
Unless it is an absolute life-or-death emergency, never log into your bank account, enter a credit card number, or access sensitive corporate files while connected to airport or coffee shop Wi-Fi. Save those tasks for when you get back to your secure home network.

### 2. Use a VPN (Virtual Private Network)
A VPN is the ultimate defense against public Wi-Fi snooping. 
When you turn on a VPN app (like Mullvad or ProtonVPN) on your phone or laptop, it scrambles all of your data into an unreadable mathematical code *before* it leaves your device. Even if you accidentally connect to a hacker's "Evil Twin" network, all they will capture is useless, scrambled gibberish.

### 3. Use Your Cellular Hotspot Instead
If you need to quickly check your bank balance or send a sensitive work email while at a coffee shop, simply turn off Wi-Fi entirely. 
Use your smartphone's 5G or 4G cellular data. Cellular networks are heavily encrypted by your phone provider and are vastly more secure than any free Wi-Fi network you will ever find. If you are on a laptop, use your phone's "Personal Hotspot" feature to share that secure cellular connection to your computer.
