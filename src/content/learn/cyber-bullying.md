## The 24/7 Nature of Digital Bullying
In previous generations, bullying generally ended when a child went home from school. Today, cyberbullying follows them into their bedrooms via Snapchat, TikTok, and group texts. Because it is digital, it can be anonymous, it can be shared instantly with hundreds of classmates, and it can happen 24 hours a day.

## Recognizing the Signs
Children are often terrified to tell their parents they are being cyberbullied because they fear their parents will solve the problem by taking their phone away. 
Instead of waiting for them to tell you, look for these behavioral changes:
* **Sudden phone secrecy:** They hide their screen when you walk into the room or jump when a notification goes off.
* **Avoidance:** They suddenly refuse to go to school or participate in activities they used to love.
* **Mood swings after screen time:** They seem perfectly fine until they spend 10 minutes on their phone, after which they become deeply depressed, angry, or withdrawn.

## The 4-Step Action Plan

If your child is being cyberbullied, do not take their phone away as a punishment, and do not respond to the bully yourself. Follow these steps:

### 1. Do Not Retaliate
Instruct your child not to reply to the messages. Bullies are looking for a reaction. Engaging in a flame war often escalates the situation and makes it harder to prove who started the harassment.

### 2. Document Everything
Before you block the bully or delete the app, **take screenshots of every single message, comment, or fake photo.** 
* Ensure the screenshots show the date, time, and the username of the bully. 
* This digital evidence is absolute gold when you need to bring the issue to the school administration or the police.

### 3. Block and Report
Once you have the evidence saved, use the platform's built-in tools.
* **Block the user:** Every app (Instagram, Roblox, iMessage) has a "Block" feature. Use it.
* **Report the behavior:** Use the app's reporting tools to flag the account for harassment. If the bully is using a fake account, the platform can permanently ban their device.

### 4. Escalate to Authorities
* **The School:** If the bully goes to the same school as your child, bring your printed screenshots to the principal. Modern schools have zero-tolerance cyberbullying policies that extend to off-campus digital behavior.
* **The Police:** If the bullying includes explicit physical threats of violence, blackmail, or the sharing of intimate photos (which is a felony in most states), take your evidence directly to local law enforcement.
