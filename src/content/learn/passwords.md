## The Core Problem: Human Memory
If you are like most people, you use the internet incorrectly. You likely have one or two "base" passwords (like `Rambo2015!` or `Springfield99`) that you use for almost every website, maybe changing the last number if the website forces you to.

**This is the single biggest security mistake you can make.**

Hackers don't guess your passwords manually. They use automated databases. If you used `Rambo2015!` on a small, insecure knitting forum in 2018, and that forum was hacked, the hackers instantly put your email and `Rambo2015!` into a giant list. Bots then take that list and try logging into banking sites, Amazon, and Gmail using that exact combination. 

If you reuse passwords, a breach on *one* terrible website instantly compromises *all* of your accounts.

## The Solution: A Password Manager
A Password Manager is a secure app on your phone and computer that acts as a digital vault. 

It does three things:
1. **Generates perfect passwords:** Instead of you creating `Password123!`, the manager generates mathematically random passwords for every website, like `xH9#pL2@qW4$mN8!`.
2. **Remembers them for you:** You no longer need to remember 100 different passwords. You only need to memorize **one** highly secure Master Password to unlock the vault. The manager remembers all the rest.
3. **Autofills them:** When you visit Netflix, the Password Manager automatically types the complex password in for you. You don't even have to look at it.

## How to Get Started Today

### 1. Choose a Reputable Manager
Do not use a notebook. Do not use an Excel file. Use an industry-trusted password manager. The top recommendations for everyday users are:
* **Bitwarden:** Excellent, completely free, and open-source.
* **1Password:** The industry gold-standard for families and businesses (costs a few dollars a month).
* **Apple Passwords / Google Password Manager:** Built directly into your phone and very easy to use, though less flexible if you switch between Apple and Android devices often.

### 2. Create your Master Password
This is the only password you ever have to remember again, so make it a "Passphrase." A Passphrase is a string of 4 or 5 random words strung together. It is incredibly easy for a human to remember, but mathematically impossible for a computer to guess.
* **Bad:** `F1d01999!` (Hard to remember, easy for a computer to guess).
* **Perfect:** `horse-battery-staple-correct` (Extremely easy to remember, impossible for a computer to guess).

### 3. Start Updating Gradually
Don't overwhelm yourself by trying to change all 200 of your accounts in one day. 
Just download the app, and going forward, whenever you log into a website, change the password to a randomly generated one and save it in your new vault. Start with your most critical accounts today: your primary Email account, and your Bank.
