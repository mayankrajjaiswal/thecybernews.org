## 1. What happened?
Security researchers discovered a massive, 12-terabyte database floating openly on the internet. This database, dubbed the "Mother of All Breaches" (MOAB), contains over **26 billion** leaked records. It is not a "new" hack of a single company, but rather a master compilation of thousands of past data breaches (like LinkedIn, Twitter, Dropbox, and Adobe) all merged into one giant, searchable file.

## 2. Am I affected?
**Almost certainly, yes.** 
Given the sheer size of the database (26 billion records), if you have been using the internet for more than a few years, it is highly likely that your email address and an old password are in this file.

## 3. Do I need to worry? (Risk Level: Medium)
You only need to worry if you **reuse the same password** across multiple websites. 

Because this database makes it incredibly easy for hackers to search for your email, they can take an old password you used for a random forum in 2018 and try to use it to log into your bank account or Amazon today.

## 4. What should I do right now?

Follow these three steps to secure yourself immediately:

1. **Check your exposure:** Go to [HaveIBeenPwned.com](https://haveibeenpwned.com/) (a safe, free tool run by security researchers) and type in your email address. It will tell you exactly which companies lost your data in the past.
2. **Change reused passwords:** If you have been using the *exact same password* for multiple websites, you must change it immediately on your most important accounts (Email, Banking, Amazon, Social Media).
3. **Turn on MFA:** Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on your primary email account (like Gmail or Outlook). If your email account is safe, hackers cannot reset passwords for your other accounts.
